    What you'll need

    • The blank canvas you'll print on. One scan belongs to one canvas.
    • Matte card or paper a little bigger than the canvas, in black or another dark or bold color. Not white or pale.
    • One solid dot on the tape that holds the canvas, from a permanent marker or a small round sticker, darker than the tape.
    • A flatbed scanner that saves PNG, JPG or TIFF at a true 600 dpi (300 dpi minimum).
    • An xTool M2 and xTool Studio on your Mac or Windows computer.

    Tip: make the preview big

    Before taking the xTool screenshot, make xTool Studio full screen, hide side panels and zoom until the canvas fills the view. The bigger the canvas, the easier the lineup.

        Your print file

        Threads onlyWhole square

        Wider paint covers the white thread fully, even if the print lands a touch off. Threads only keeps ink out of the holes; whole square paints the holes too.

        The PNG is the same print at 400 dpi with a transparent background, the same size as the SVG. Recolor or erase in xTool Studio if you like, but don't resize, crop or rotate it. Its edges line up the way the magenta frame does.

        In xTool Studio

        1. Import the SVG. Don't resize it. It's already real size in millimeters, with a magenta frame around it.
        2. Drag the magenta frame onto the stitched preview. Zoom right in on one corner and line it up exactly. Never rotate or scale it, and don't open the lid or take a new preview until it's placed.
        3. Check with a Close shot or two, aimed at the spot you're checking. Turn color layers off and on to check each one. A few arrow-key nudges are fine.
        4. Delete the magenta frame, then print.
